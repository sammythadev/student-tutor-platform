===== SLIDE 4 =====
  1.1 BACKGROUND
  A Large Market, an Informal Matching Process
  Secondary-school tutoring demand in Nigeria is driven by exam preparation, not enrichment.
  Online tutoring reached USD 10.4 bn in 2024 and is forecast to nearly double to USD 23.7 bn by 2030 (Grand View Research, 2025); primary and secondary learners drive over half of that demand.
  WASSCE 2024: 72.12% of 1.8 m candidates scored credit passes in at least five subjects - a 7.69-point drop from 2023, leaving roughly half a million candidates short in core subjects (Vanguard, 2024).
  Personalisation, not content, drives engagement in educational recommender systems (Deschenes, 2020; da Silva et al., 2023; Spivakovsky et al., 2025).
  Deployed matching is primitive: manual assignment, keyword filtering, or rules over subject and availability only (Taveekarn et al., 2014; Ramesh, 2020).
  The ranking effect concentrates demand on popular tutors while new tutors idle (Pitoura et al., 2022), and calendars collide.
  Why a matching problem, not a search problem
  Assigning n students to m tutors with capacity limits and conflicting preferences is constrained combinatorial optimisation. Weighted scoring resolves the criteria trade-off; a priority-queue greedy pass with lazy fairness recomputation resolves the allocation at scale.

===== SLIDE 5 =====
  1.2 STATEMENT OF PROBLEM
  Four Defects in Every Current Platform
  01
  Limited matching criteria
  Tutor-finder platforms match on subject and time slot and little else. Teaching style, language compatibility, pricing and historical performance are ignored (Taveekarn et al., 2014; Ramesh, 2020).
  02
  Scheduling conflicts
  A tutor's calendar and a student's calendar often fail to overlap, and the same conflict recurs weekly for anyone on a fixed timetable.
  03
  Uneven tutor workload
  A few well-ranked tutors are flooded with requests while the rest sit idle - the ranking effect concentrates supply instead of distributing it (Pitoura et al., 2022).
  04
  Opaque recommendations
  A failed match costs hours of contact and real money from the paying family, and neither side can see why the pairing was proposed.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
  2.2 - 2.4
  Three Frames, One Unanswered Question
  Conceptual framework
  Multi-criteria decision-making resolves conflicting suitability criteria through explicit weights (Wang et al., 2023; Hien et al., 2025).
  Weighted scoring models give a single comparable number per student-tutor pair.
  Theoretical framework
  Greedy bipartite matching carries a proven half-approximation guarantee - used directly as the design basis rather than cited as background.
  The guarantee covers the static-only variant; the shipped engine's fairness term, top-K truncation and hash tie-break fall outside its assumptions.
  Empirical framework
  Educational recommender systems overwhelmingly recommend content or resources, not people (da Silva et al., 2023).
  Direct tutor allocation is attempted rarely, and where it exists it optimises stability or a single axis.
  THE RESEARCH GAP
  No reviewed system combines a multi-criterion weighted compatibility score, a hard availability constraint, and a load-balancing objective in a single assignment decision - and none of them report their output against an exact optimal-assignment oracle.
  This work closes that gap: it scores every eligible pair, allocates under capacity, prices fairness explicitly, and validates all three against a min-cost-max-flow oracle and three baseline strategies over 30 independent populations per scenario.

===== SLIDE 10 =====
  4.5.1
  The Composite Scoring Model
  a.A(s,t)  +  b.P(s,t)  +  c.S(s,t)  +  d.F(t)          a + b + c + d = 1 ,  each sub-score in [0,1]
  A - Academic score
  1.0 exact subject + specialisation match
  0.7 subject matches, stated specialisations do not align
  0.5 subject matches, specialisation data missing
  Exam-type term: 1.0 supported or unspecified, 0.5 explicit mismatch
  Grade-gap term g penalises distance from the nearest supported grade level
  P - Preference score
  Style: cosine similarity over one-hot vectors of delivery mode, format and learning style
  Budget: 1 if the hourly rate is within budget, linear decay on excess
  Region: 1 for online or same-region in-person, 0 for mismatch, 0.5 when data is missing
  S - Schedule score
  Overlap between requested slots H_s and available slots H_t
  One contiguous tutor slot must cover the request; fragments across several slots fail
  A student with zero declared availability is rejected as an incomplete profile
  Fairness term  F(t) = (1 - load(t)/capacity(t))^1.15 + 0.05
  - the 1.15 exponent makes the penalty on heavily loaded tutors slightly super-linear; the 0.05 cold-start boost applies only to a tutor with zero assigned students, nudging unused tutors back into circulation. Default load-factor weight d = 0.05.

===== SLIDE 11 =====
  4.5.2
  Priority-Queue Greedy with Lazy Fairness Recompute
  1
  Hard eligibility filter
  The tutor must teach one of the student's subjects, support the grade level and exam type, and have spare capacity. Ineligible pairs are never scored and never reach the heap.
  2
  Push eligible pairs
  Each eligible (student, tutor) pair is scored by the composite model and pushed onto a max-heap keyed by priority.
  3
  Pop the best pair
  The highest-priority pair is assigned, consuming one seat from that tutor's capacity.
  4
  Recompute lazily
  Because a tutor's load changed, every remaining pair touching that tutor is stale: pop it, re-score it with the fresh F(t), push it back.
  5
  Mark the rest unassignable
  When the heap empties with students still unplaced, each is recorded with a structured reason code - never silently dropped.
  Why lazy recompute
  Static sorting has a known flaw: a tutor's fairness score is correct only while the tutor has no students. The moment a seat is taken, every cached score for that tutor is stale. Lazy recompute re-pushes only the affected pairs instead of re-sorting the whole candidate set - the fairness term stays exact at a fraction of the cost.
  Complexity
  Time: O(S.T) to score eligible pairs, O(E log E) for heap operations, plus lazy re-pushes - O(n^2 log n) overall
  Space: O(E) heap entries, one per live candidate pair
  Measured: 5,000 students / 500 tutors gives 208,336 pairs scored in 2.28 s mean

  4.2 - 4.4
  Three-Tier Architecture, Algorithm in the Domain Layer
  Presentation tier
  React front end - tutor, student and admin dashboards - consumes POST /api/matches - renders explainable match reasons
  ▶
  Application tier
  NestJS controllers - DTO validation at the boundary - services orchestrate scoring and assignment - typed HTTP exceptions, never bare throws
  ▶
  Domain layer
  CompositeScorer - GreedyAssignmentEngine (max-heap + lazy fairness) - match rationale builder - capacity and eligibility policy
  ▶
  Data tier
  PostgreSQL via Drizzle ORM - schema-first - users, profiles, subjects, availability, capacity, assignments - zero N+1 joins
  End-to-end matchmaking flow
  Controller validates the request and loads the eligible population
  Scorer produces a compatibility score and a per-criterion breakdown
  Engine allocates under capacity, then runs bounded augmenting-path repair
  Service returns placements, unplaced reason codes and human-readable rationales
  Edge cases handled explicitly
  Incomplete profiles rejected: a student with no declared availability never enters the pool
  Zero-capacity tutors filtered before scoring, not after
  Fragmented slot coverage fails the contiguous-slot rule
  Unassignable students carry a structured reason, never a silent drop

  4.10.1
  The Evaluation Harness
  core/evaluation/evaluation-harness.ts generates synthetic student and tutor populations of configurable size and student:tutor ratio - no live production data required, consistent with the stated limitation in Section 1.6.
  Three scenario families: realistic-seed (50:50, mirroring the platform's actual Nigerian secondary-school seed data and capacity distribution), moderate-* (1.5:1 to 4:1, probing fairness where slack exists but is not unlimited), and stress-sweep (a deliberate 10:1 oversubscription test at four scales).
  30 independent populations per scenario, exploratory seeds 0-29. Every reported mean carries a 95% confidence interval, and every engine-versus-baseline claim is backed by a paired sign test.
  An exact min-cost-max-flow oracle provides the reference point, and the engine is compared against three deployed-style strategies: filter-only FCFS, score-based self-selection, and student-proposing deferred acceptance.

  4.10.2  ·  NEGATIVE RESULT
  A Real Defect, With Proven Headroom
  The worst-placed student still scores only 0.380-0.433 across the unsaturated scenarios while the mean sits near 0.59-0.62. One badly served student is invisible to an average.
  Repair does not fix it: the augmenting-path pass adds students, it does not re-rank the ones already seated. The floor is structural, not a leak.
  An exact theta-constrained solver puts the largest achievable floor at 0.4059-0.4101 across the unsaturated scenarios - 0.0075 to 0.0241 above the repaired engine's own static floor.
  The price of that fairness is at most 0.0003 of static total per student, inside the pre-registered 0.002 negligibility band, while the exact arm never loses a population on total, coverage or the worst-student metric.
  This is a defect in the shipped engine, not a market bound. It is stated as such in the report's correction addendum.

===== SLIDE 22 =====
  4.10.6
  Explainability as a Design Requirement
  A match a family cannot interrogate is a match they will not trust.
  The scorer returns a per-criterion breakdown, not only a composite number: academic fit, preference fit, schedule overlap and the tutor's live load factor are each exposed to the consumer.
  Every unplaced student carries a structured reason code - no tutor eligible, all eligible tutors at capacity, no contiguous slot overlap - so a student sees why, instead of an empty result.
  The fairness term is surfaced as a tutor-load indicator, which makes the load-balancing behaviour legible to an administrator rather than mysterious.
  Rationale text is generated server-side alongside the assignment, so the explanation cannot drift from the computation that produced it.
  Why this matters in this domain
  The motivating failure is a paying family losing hours of contact and money on a match that never worked. An explanation is the cheapest available defence against that, and it costs one breakdown object per assignment.

===== SLIDE 24 =====
  5.1 - 5.2
  Research Questions, Answered
  RQ1
  Is the scoring model effective?
  Yes, decisively. Against filter-only FCFS it adds 0.067-0.182 of mean score at every ratio (p < 0.0001), and it is the only mechanism here that accounts for style, language, budget and region at all.
  RQ2
  Does capacity-aware assignment win under contention?
  Not where supply is abundant - self-selection is indistinguishable from the engine at 1:1 and 1.5:1 (p = 0.86). From 2:1 onward the engine leads by 0.019 at 3:1 and 0.124 at 10:1, and places more students in every unsaturated scenario.
  RQ3
  What is the measured optimality gap?
  97.61% at the worst tested instance before repair; 98.52-99.75% of the oracle's static total after it, with coverage within 0.0004-0.0011 of optimal. Empirical, not a proven bound.
  RQ4
  What is the price of fairness?
  Small and measurable. Load-aware fairness costs 0.012-0.023 in mean score at 2:1-3:1 while raising Jain's index from 0.714 to 0.730 and lifting coverage. Protecting the worst student costs at most 0.0003 per student.

===== SLIDE 25 =====
  5.3 - 5.5
  Recommendations, Contribution and Future Work
  Recommendations
  Ship top-K = 20 as the default candidate-list size - the quality knee, at a fraction of infinity's scoring cost.
  Adopt the theta-constrained exact floor as a nightly offline recomputation, not a request-path step.
  Keep the load factor weight at d = 0.05; the sweep shows no higher setting pays for itself.
  Contribution to knowledge
  A scoring-plus-availability matching model whose fairness term is made exact at run time by lazy recomputation, rather than approximated by static sorting.
  An honest account of two claims that did not survive scrutiny - the half-approximation and the 'greedy places 59 of 64' figure - rescoped and reported as empirical.
  Future work
  01
  Replace the static composite with a learned compatibility model, keeping the fairness term exogenous so the guarantee is not traded away for accuracy.
  02
  Extend the theta-constraint from the worst student to a full equity profile - per-region, per-subject and per-income-band placement floors.
  03
  Replace seeded synthetic populations with the platform's own anonymised assignment history, and re-run the same 30-population protocol as a live A/B test.
